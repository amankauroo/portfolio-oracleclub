My Journey into Robotics with Arduino and ESP32

August 2026

← Back to Blog

My journey into robotics began with the Arduino Uno, a platform that introduced me to the exciting world of electronics and programming. Like many beginners, my very first project was the classic Blink program. Although it was only a simple 5 mm LED turning on and off through the Arduino IDE, it was the moment that sparked my passion for embedded systems. Seeing a few lines of code control a physical component inspired me to explore what else was possible with microcontrollers.

As my knowledge grew, I started experimenting with a wide variety of sensors and electronic modules. I worked with the DHT11 and DHT22 temperature and humidity sensors, the MQ-2 gas sensor, the HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor for distance measurement, and the LCD 1602 display with an I²C module to present sensor data. Every new component introduced me to different communication protocols and programming techniques, allowing me to build increasingly sophisticated projects while strengthening my understanding of electronics and embedded programming.

One of my favourite projects was building a Bluetooth-controlled robotic car using the Arduino Uno, an L293D motor driver shield, four DC motors, and the HC-05 Bluetooth module. Controlling the vehicle wirelessly from a smartphone gave me valuable experience in motor control, wireless communication, and power management. I also designed a temperature data logger that continuously recorded sensor readings onto an SD card using an SD card module, introducing me to data logging and file management on embedded systems.

As I continued learning, I explored additional hardware including servo motors, joysticks, the MPU6500 motion sensor for measuring acceleration and rotational movement, and many other Arduino-compatible sensors and modules. Working with these devices helped me understand motion sensing, sensor calibration, and real-world applications of embedded systems. Each project challenged me to solve practical problems and improve both my programming and hardware design skills.

Today, I am expanding my knowledge by working with the ESP32, a significantly more powerful microcontroller than the Arduino Uno. Running at up to 240 MHz, compared to the Arduino Uno’s 16 MHz, the ESP32 provides built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth connectivity, opening the door to Internet of Things (IoT) applications. With these capabilities, I can develop projects that communicate over the internet, host small web servers, send sensor data to websites, and remotely monitor or control devices. Exploring the ESP32 has taken my robotics journey to the next level and continues to inspire me to build smarter, more connected systems.
